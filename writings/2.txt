Don't make more decisions than your edge can pay for

Making decisions is a part of everyone's life. We make decisions on a daily basis. Have you ever wondered how these decisions are evaluated in your brain. Daniel Kahneman defines this as the source of decisions as being system 1 and system 2. System 1 is that part of the brain that takes decisions on a fight or flight mode. It is that part of the brain that comes into action at the most required time. System 2 is the lazy part of the brain that takes time to evaluate stuff. So the brain always tries to avoid System 2 and hands over control to System 1. 

I always have a rule or maybe I'd have read this somewhere (I dont remember). Never take a decision when you are emotionally high or low. Taking a decision when you are happy increases your assumption of the probability of success in your decisions meaning you just omit the downsides of your decisions. The opposite happens when you take a decision when you are sad. Tell yourself to "Wait". This is most required in the Public Equity Markets. You will pay a heavy price if you fall for the system 1 thinking. And that price is "Regret". A decent investor knows for a fact that one cannot hide from this, but can only optimize to minimize regrets. Its a classic Linear Programming Problem but within your brain. But think of it, its not a single variable that you have to optimize, you still have to maximize your returns and minimize your risks! That is a multi-objective optimization problem. And people think they can crack it on their first day! Markets demands your time and patience. You shall pay your tuition fee to the markets in the first 3-4 years or atleast in the first cycle( a bull and a bear market). 

When you make a lot of decisions of buying and selling, your returns fall. One of the resasons is that you pay the STT in India and the brokerage charges. The other is that you have a finite capacity to probabilistically make the right decisions. That was the reason why Steve Jobs used to where the same kinda Jeans and a black turtle neck shirt everyday. He didn't want to wake up in the morning and decide in front of his wardrobe what to wear because he had a whole day in front of him to decide which feature on your iphone is best for you so you like it! He wanted to make some finite and important decisions. 

Mathematically, 
Say you make n trades. Each trade k has an expected edge eₖ (what you'd make on average, before costs) and a cost c (STT, brokerage, stamp duty, GST, slippage). Your expected total return is:

E[R] = Σ (eₖ − c), summed over k = 1 to n

Adding one more trade helps only if its edge exceeds its cost: eₖ > c. Every trade that fails this test makes you poorer in expectation. Mood inflates your estimate of eₖ, so you take trades that look like they pass eₖ > c but don't. "Wait" is a margin of safety on your own probability estimate.

We have a finite capacity to make good decisions,  that is why Warren Buffett told about the 20 hole punch card idea. Imagine you get a card with only 20 punches for your entire lifetime, and every investment you make uses one. Once they're used up, you can't invest again. Buffett argued that under this rule you'd think very carefully about each decision, only act on your best ideas, and load up heavily when you did, and you'd end up much richer. So, eₖ decreases with k increasing assuming that your best decision are in the beginning and then the quality decreases along with the return.
